The Aegis Node.

The permanent, solar-autonomous backbone of the mesh network. Engineered for indefinite operation in zero-infrastructure environments.

// FIG 1.0: MODULAR INTERNAL ARCHITECTURE

Anatomy of Resilience

A breakdown of the key subsystems that allow the Node to operate without a grid.

  • Solar-Ready Enclosure (IP67) Ruggedized, UV-resistant polymer case with integrated mounts for a 20W solar panel. Gasket-sealed to protect against dust and temporary submersion.
  • High-Gain LoRa Antenna Tuned for the 915/868 MHz band. Provides the 15km+ operational range. Mounted externally for maximum line-of-sight propagation.
  • Core Logic Board (ARM Cortex) The brain. A low-power, quad-core processor handling packet routing, encryption, and the local Wi-Fi bridge for civilian connection.
  • LoRa Transceiver Module (SX1262) The heart of the mesh. This module handles the long-range physics, sending and receiving "chirp" spread spectrum signals below the noise floor.
  • Massive LiFePO4 Battery Bank 80,000mAh of energy storage. LiFePO4 chemistry is chosen for its safety, long cycle life (2000+ cycles), and stability in extreme temperatures.

Why 80,000mAh?

Most IoT devices are built for "sunny day" scenarios. We build for nuclear winter. A disaster can cover a region in ash or heavy clouds for weeks, dropping solar efficiency to near zero.

The Aegis Node's massive battery reservoir ensures that the network's backbone remains alive and beaconing for half a year in total darkness. It is the difference between a network that fails when it's needed most, and one that endures.
